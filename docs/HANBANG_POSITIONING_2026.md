## One-Line Position

Wedding Hanbang is the transparent transaction layer for the Korean wedding market.

Korean positioning:

> 예비부부가 손해 보지 않고, 투명한 업체가 더 좋은 고객을 만나는 AI 웨딩 거래 신뢰 인프라.

This is not simply an AI wedding planner, a vendor marketplace, or an all-in-one wedding checklist app. The core product promise is a safer, clearer transaction before money changes hands.

## Founder Mission

We are not trying to own every part of the wedding market. We are trying to lead the part that should not be compromised:

> 결혼시장에서 소비자가 정보 비대칭 때문에 손해 보거나 피해를 보면 안 된다.

The second half of the mission is just as important:

> 정직하고 투명한 업체가 광고비나 불투명한 영업 방식이 아니라 실력과 신뢰로 더 좋은 고객을 만나야 한다.

This mission creates a sharper product strategy than "more features." The product should help consumers make safer decisions before contracts, deposits, extra fees, and irreversible scheduling commitments, while helping good vendors earn trust without opaque platform games.

## Strategic Interpretation

The best opportunity is not "do what competitors do not do." Some existing features are useful, and Wedding Hanbang can do them better when they reinforce trust.

The right filter is:

> Does this feature make the wedding transaction clearer, safer, and more efficient for both the couple and a transparent vendor?

If yes, it can belong in the product even if competitors also have a version of it. If no, it should be delayed even if it looks attractive.

## First Principles

### 1. The wedding market is structurally asymmetric.

Consumers are usually first-time buyers. Vendors and planners are repeat sellers. The seller knows the true price structure, negotiable parts, refund norms, and common traps. The consumer does not.

The product should therefore start from asymmetry, not convenience.

### 2. The expensive moment is before the contract, not after the checklist.

Many wedding apps help after the couple has already chosen a direction. Wedding Hanbang should win at the earlier, sharper moment:

> "Can I sign this estimate or contract without getting hurt?"

This is a stronger wedge than general planning.

### 3. Trust is the product, not a brand adjective.

"Consumer-side" must be visible in the mechanics:

- show evidence
- show sample count
- show source type
- say "cannot determine" when data is insufficient
- separate public data, user-submitted data, vendor-provided data, and AI inference
- never rank vendors by hidden paid placement

### 4. AI is not the moat by itself.

The moat is a structured trust graph:

- estimate line items
- optional/hidden fees
- contract clauses
- vendor response patterns
- negotiation outcomes
- verified reviews
- post-contract actual costs

AI is the interface and reasoning layer over this graph. Without the graph, the product becomes a generic chatbot.

### 5. Community should collect evidence, not just opinions.

Free-form posts are useful, but not defensible. A defensible community turns real user experience into structured market data.

Every quote-check post should become an anonymized estimate card. Every contract concern should become a clause pattern. Every vendor answer should update the vendor trust file.

### 6. Win-win comes from reducing uncertainty, not from squeezing one side.

A bad wedding platform extracts value by selling exposure, hiding ranking logic, or pushing mismatched leads. A good wedding platform creates value by reducing wasted time, unclear expectations, and avoidable disputes.

Consumers win when they understand the true cost, risk, and questions before signing. Vendors win when they meet better-prepared customers who understand their package, budget, and fit.

## What We Should Not Be

1. Not a generic wedding super-app at first.
   - 청첩장, 축의금, 하객관리, 스토어, 여행, 혼수까지 다 하려고 하면 focus is diluted.

2. Not a paid-ranking vendor marketplace.
   - This conflicts with the consumer-side promise too early.

3. Not a points-for-reviews community clone.
   - Incentivized posting can grow content volume while weakening trust.

4. Not an ungrounded AI advisor.
   - Hallucinated average prices, legal conclusions, or vendor claims would destroy the brand.

5. Not an anti-vendor attack product.
   - The long-term market must reward transparent vendors, not simply shame vendors.

6. Not a consumer-only complaint board.
   - Consumer protection is the entry point, but the market gets healthier only when fair vendors can participate and prove their standards.

## Product Architecture

### Consumer Surface

1. Quote Risk Check
   - Input: category, region, quote amount, included items, excluded items, vendor name optional, screenshot/document optional.
   - Output: risk grade, missing items, likely extra fees, benchmark range, questions to ask, confidence level.

2. Contract Clause Check
   - Input: contract text, refund/cancellation clause, date-change clause, original file clause, extra fee clause.
   - Output: plain Korean explanation, risk markers, questions to confirm, "human review recommended" flag.

3. Anonymous Estimate Card
   - Structured community card for price comparison.
   - Fields: region, date, category, guest count if hall, vendor type, quoted amount, included items, extra fees, contract status.

4. Vendor Trust File
   - Public-facing vendor intelligence page.
   - Shows verified official info, community estimate distribution, common extra-fee questions, response transparency, complaint-risk patterns.

5. Amora Negotiation Coach
   - Generates vendor questions.
   - Interprets vendor replies.
   - Detects evasive answers.
   - Saves next actions to the user's dashboard.

### Community Surface

Keep tools that are immediate, shallow, and shareable:

- quote check
- contract phrase check
- "what should I ask this vendor?" generator
- anonymize-before-posting helper
- vendor tag summary
- script/thank-you wording tone clinic

Do not put deep private workspaces in community:

- invitation management
- gift-money ledger
- guest management
- private couple chat
- full budget operation
- full schedule operation
- full script editor

### Dashboard Surface

The dashboard is the user's private execution room:

- saved quote reports
- contract questions
- budget impact
- schedule tasks
- Amora action memory
- invitations
- gift-money ledger
- guestbook
- long-form scripts

### Vendor Surface

The vendor surface should start as a transparency and lead-quality product, not an ad product:

- official vendor profile
- verified price range
- included/excluded item standards
- extra-fee policy
- refund/date-change policy
- frequently asked consumer questions
- response templates
- official correction channel
- qualified inquiry inbox

The vendor product should help transparent vendors explain themselves clearly, reduce repetitive 상담, and avoid mismatched inquiries.

## Defensive Moat

The strongest moat is not feature count. It is compounding trust data.

### Data Moat

Wedding Hanbang should standardize the messy wedding market into durable data objects:

- EstimateItem
- HiddenFee
- ContractClause
- VendorAnswer
- NegotiationOutcome
- VerifiedActualCost
- TrustSignal
- RiskPattern

### Community Moat

The community should produce comparable data, not only content.

Example:

> "스드메 280만원 괜찮나요?"

Should become:

- category: SDM package
- region: Seoul
- quote: 2,800,000 KRW
- includes: studio, dress, makeup
- excludes: original files, dress upgrade, helper fee
- risk: missing original-file cost
- community responses: 18
- similar estimate cards: 43

### Trust Moat

Trust grows when the product refuses bad money:

- no hidden paid ranking
- no vendor override of consumer evidence
- no AI price claim without source tier
- public correction path for vendors
- audit log for AI-generated claims

### Vendor Moat

Transparent vendors should eventually join because Wedding Hanbang sends better-prepared, higher-intent customers and gives them a fair way to prove trust.

Vendor value should be:

- verified transparent profile
- fewer repetitive questions
- better-qualified leads
- consumer question analytics
- trust badge
- official correction channel
- clearer package explanation
- lower mismatch rate
- public proof that their quote is understandable and complete

Do not start by charging vendors for ranking. Start by letting good vendors prove transparency.

The long-term business model should monetize workflow, qualification, verification, and transaction safety before paid placement:

- vendor verification
- qualified lead routing
- contract/quote workflow tools
- transparency badge program
- dispute-prevention documentation
- eventually transaction/payment tools only after trust is proven

## Go-To-Market

### Consumer Acquisition Wedge

The strongest top-of-funnel message:

> "계약금 보내기 전, 이 견적이 정상인지 3분 안에 확인하세요."

Best acquisition channels:

- SEO around "스드메 견적 정상", "드레스 추가금", "웨딩홀 보증인원", "원본비", "헬퍼비", "계약금 환불"
- shareable quote-check reports
- anonymous community estimate cards
- Instagram/Reels/TikTok educational clips
- Naver cafe/community posts that answer one concrete risk
- free downloadable "contract before deposit checklist"

### Activation Loop

1. User uploads or enters quote.
2. Amora returns risk report.
3. User sees questions to ask vendor.
4. User shares anonymized card to community.
5. Community adds context.
6. User saves report to dashboard.
7. User comes back with vendor response or contract clause.
8. Data improves.

### Vendor Acquisition Loop

1. Consumer traffic reveals repeated vendor questions.
2. Vendor sees unofficial but fair trust file.
3. Vendor claims profile and provides official transparent info.
4. Profile gets verified transparency label.
5. Consumers prefer vendors with clearer info.
6. More vendors join to avoid being absent or unclear.

This loop should be framed positively:

> "좋은 업체가 불필요한 오해 없이, 준비된 고객을 만나는 곳."

Not:

> "나쁜 업체를 잡아내는 곳."

## Hallucination Policy

This product cannot tolerate hallucination. The AI must follow these rules:

1. If sample count is insufficient, say so.
2. If only public average data is available, do not pretend it matches the user's exact quote.
3. Never say a clause is illegal unless backed by reviewed legal source or human review.
4. Use "risk," "needs confirmation," and "ask this question" more than definitive accusations.
5. Separate AI inference from user-submitted evidence.
6. Show source tier on every price comparison.
7. Do not generate vendor-specific claims without evidence.
8. Escalate high-risk cases to human review or "do not rely solely on AI" messaging.

## 2026-05-11 Product Surface Update

The community first screen should now communicate the trust loop directly:

1. Quote entered.
2. Missing cost and contract-risk items detected.
3. Vendor questions generated.
4. Anonymous estimate card shared.
5. Comparable evidence improves the next diagnosis.
6. Transparent vendors earn trust through clearer terms.

This is the current product wedge in one sentence:

> 기능을 많이 가진 웨딩 앱이 아니라, 계약 전 판단을 더 안전하게 만드는 견적 신뢰 운영체제.

Design and navigation decisions:

- Community hero leads with quote trust, not generic social posting.
- Community tools stay shallow and shareable: quote check, Amora questions, anonymous estimate cards, checkpoints.
- Deep private operations stay in the service body: budget, schedule, invitations, money, scripts.
- Vendor messaging stays positive: "transparent vendors are rewarded," not "vendors are punished."
- Quote-check results should always lead to a next action: copy questions, ask Amora, share anonymously, or save to dashboard.

## Strategic Verdict

The strongest strategy is not to become the biggest wedding feature bundle. The strongest strategy is to become the most trusted place before a wedding consumer and vendor commit to each other.

Wedding Hanbang should lead with consumer protection, build a structured trust graph from real quote and contract data, and invite transparent vendors into that system as early as the trust rules are clear.

If we do this well, the company can influence the market without trying to own every transaction. The prize is not "all wedding features." The prize is becoming the default trust checkpoint of the Korean wedding market: the place couples check before they pay, and the place transparent vendors want to be verified.
